Validation

The reader is checked against the optimal values published for the test collections of the literature: each instance is read, its deterministic equivalent solved with HiGHS, and its optimal value compared with the published one, to the digits published (give or take 1e-9 of the value, the accuracy of the solve).

The instances

  • POSTS, the portable stochastic programming test set of Holmes (1995): pltexp, fxm, storm and sgpf, in two to six periods, with the values of its file Post_Results.
  • Felt's test set (Ariyawansa and Felt, 2004): airlift, assets, cargo, chem, LandS (in two and three periods), environ, phone, stocfor, with the solutions distributed with it and the values of Zverovich's thesis (2011, Table 4.3).
  • GENSLP and SAPHIR (rand, saphir): the values of Zverovich's thesis.
  • SIPLIB, the stochastic integer programming test library of Ahmed et al.: SSLP, SIZES, DCAP and SEMI, with the optimal values, or the best bounds, of its pages, and for DCAP those of Kim and Dandurand (2022); SMKP, with the optimal values of Kim and Dandurand.
  • COIN-OR SMI's multistage instances (KandW3R, app0110R, wat10C_32), with the values of Zehtabian and Bastin (CIRRELT-2016-12, Table 2).
  • WATSON, the pension fund problems of Consigli and Dempster (1998), in ten periods and 16 to 2688 scenarios, with the values of their Tables 2 and 3, and of Dempster and Thompson (1998) for wati-10-64.
  • The sampling instances of Linderoth, Shapiro and Wright (2006): gbd, LandS, 20term, ssn and storm, of 646,425 to 6×10^81 scenarios, whose optimal values they estimate from samples; Bayraksan and Morton (2011) give gbd's.
  • Topaloglu's datasets (Cornell ORIE): electricity planning, fleet sizing and product distribution, of 10^47 to 10^204 scenarios.

The files are those of SPLIB (Zverovich's collection), of COIN-OR's Data-Stochastic and of DSP (for SMKP), each downloaded at a fixed commit and checked by the hash of its content, and Topaloglu's zip files, checked by their SHA-256. The instances, their files and their published values are listed in validation/instances.toml, with the reference of each value.

An instance whose optimal value is not published, or whose deterministic equivalent is out of reach, is only read: 20term, ssn and storm, whose 10^12 to 10^81 scenarios leave only the estimates of Linderoth, Shapiro and Wright, and Topaloglu's datasets, for which the values of two approximate policies are published, not optimal values. Reading them checks the files, not a value. gbd and LandS have deterministic equivalents of tens of millions of nonzeros (huge): gbd's optimum is that of Bayraksan and Morton (2011), who solve it exactly; LandS's, which is not published, must lie within the confidence intervals of the estimates.

The collections that the Stochastic Programming Society lists are all here, but for a few that the reader cannot check:

  • the random right-hand side instances of Trapp, Prokopyev and Schaefer (2013): their page is gone, and the paper reports run times, not optimal values;
  • SIPLIB's VACCINE, chance-constrained: its time files start both periods at the same column, which SMPS cannot express, and the chance constraint is not in the files; MPTSPs, PROBPORT and EXPUTIL are not in SMPS (a format of their own, the MPS file of a deterministic equivalent, a nonlinear objective);
  • SMKP's instances 21 to 30, for which no optimal value is published.

Running it

julia --project=validation -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.develop(path = "."); Pkg.instantiate()'
julia --project=validation validation/validate.jl --size medium --output results.md

--size selects the instances by the time their deterministic equivalent takes: small (seconds; the continuous integration runs them), medium (minutes), large (more, and a few gigabytes of memory), huge (over ten million nonzeros, more memory than most computers have). --only selects them by name, --time-limit limits each solve. The script exits with an error when an instance differs from its published value without a known reason, or cannot be read or solved.

What it shows

The published values are reproduced, with a few exceptions, each documented in the results and in instances.toml:

  • Probabilities as written. Some stoch files give probabilities with a few digits, which do not sum up to 1: 1/6 as 0.1667, whose sum is 1.0002, or 0.166667, 1/15 as 0.066667. The published values of POSTS (pltexpA216, pltexpB3, pltexpB4), of Zverovich's thesis (airlift's randgen; assets-large, whose probabilities sum up to 1.0000019; the rand instances in 6000 scenarios, one of whose blocks has six probabilities 0.166667) and of SIPLIB (sslp154515) were computed with them as written, without normalizing them, and are reproduced with probability_sums = :as_written.
  • Values computed on other files. PostResults differs from the values computed on SPLIB's files of fxm and sgpf by other authors, with other readers (Felt's for fxm, COIN-OR SMI's for sgpf5y4), which agree with this one; the difference is the same on the larger instances of the family, for which no other value is published. SPLIB's sgpf3y files are not those of POSTS, as Felt notes. PostResults' values of stormG2.8, stormG2.125 and stormG2.1000 are 3.8, 1.0 and 0.55 below HiGHS's, whatever its tolerances and method. SIPLIB's value of sizes3 is the optimum of the deterministic equivalent distributed with the files, whose costs, weighted by the probabilities 1/3, are rounded to two decimals. Where a value computed on the same files agrees, it is the reference, and the note gives the collection's.
  • Felt's solutions of environ. The solutions distributed with env-15, env-8232, env-aggr and env-loose are those of env-diss.cor, not env.cor, with the zeros of the last block of the stoch file read as the values of its first block: Felt's solver seems to have skipped the zeros of the stoch file. So read, the files give his values exactly. Zverovich's values, which read the zeros, are the reference of environ.
  • A maximization. saphir's core file has no OBJSENSE, but its objective gives costs the coefficient -1: as a minimization, each of its scenarios is unbounded. Read with sense = :max (the key sense of instances.toml), it gives Zverovich's values.
  • The solver. HiGHS's default method, the simplex method after its presolve, does not end within 1800 seconds on the largest deterministic equivalents: 4node and 4node-base from 8192 scenarios, phone, rand from rand0-6000 on (nor did CPLEX's, in Zverovich's Table 4.3), gbd and LandS. The key highs of instances.toml gives these instances options of HiGHS, which the results note: here its interior point method, without presolve or crossover, which solves gbd in five minutes and LandS in twenty. On pltexpA66, HiGHS 1.15.1's presolve returns as optimal a feasible point of value -27.9130197; without it, HiGHS finds PostResults' optimum, -28.134408. None of HiGHS's methods solves 4node-base-16384 within 1800 seconds. Its interior point methods make no progress on saphir, whose simplex method solves saphir-200 in 20 minutes, but not saphir-500 and saphir-1000 within 1800 seconds.
  • Zverovich's tolerance. Zverovich's values of env and env-diss in 8232 and 32928 scenarios, whose probabilities are exact, are above the optimum by 6.4e-6 to 9.2e-6 of the value, more than half a unit of their sixth digit; those of saphir-50 and saphir-100, a maximization, are below it by 4.8e-6 and 5.0e-6. That is within the relative stopping tolerance of his decomposition methods, 1e-5 (p. 72), whose stopping point is a feasible solution, worse than the optimum. HiGHS gives the same values by the simplex method and by interior points.
  • The accuracy of the solvers. pltexpA56 is 1.7e-6 above PostResults' value, beyond its last digit, while the other pltexpA instances agree; HiGHS gives the same value by the simplex method with tighter tolerances, without presolve or scaling, and by interior points. The pltexp problems are ill-conditioned: on the same deterministic equivalents, HiGHS and Clp differ by 2e-6 to 1e-5 on pltexpB3 to pltexpB5, more than this difference.
  • Unexplained difference. pltexpB5 is 0.0028 above Post_Results' value with its probabilities as written (-23.843400 against -23.846166), 0.0066 with them normalized, while pltexpB3 and pltexpB4, read the same way, agree. COIN-OR SMI reads the same problem from its files (see below).
  • Integer programs. SIPLIB gives the optimal values of SSLP and SIZES, and only bounds for DCAP and SEMI: the optimum found must lie within them. When a solve stops at its time limit, its bounds must be consistent with the published ones. SIPLIB's bounds of DCAP, those of the DBB method of Ahmed, Tawarmalani and Sahinidis (2004), are above the optimal values of four of the five instances in 200 and 500 scenarios that Kim and Dandurand (2022) solve; the solve here finds solutions of their values. Their values are the reference of those instances. In 300 scenarios, whose probabilities are written 0.003333 and sum up to 0.9999, Kim and Dandurand's values are the optimal values times 0.9999, as if the first-stage costs too were weighted by the probabilities as written; SIPLIB's bounds are the reference, but on dcap332300 and dcap342300, whose optima, 1252.876909 and at most 2067.702735, are below SIPLIB's lower bounds, 1253.040 and 2067.777. HiGHS does not close the gap of SMKP's instances within minutes: their bounds are checked against Kim and Dandurand's optima.
  • WATSON's files. Consigli and Dempster's solvers agree to the digits they print, at least two decimals, which is the tolerance of their values. Two of SPLIB's 24 files are not those of the published values: watc-10-64.sto is the same file as wati-10-64.sto, and wati-10-1920.sto starts with an initial cash balance of 500.63 instead of 300.63 (with 300.63, the published value is reproduced). The probabilities of watc-10-1920.sto sum up to 1.2, those of 1536 equally likely scenarios given to 1536 of its 1920; normalized, they give the published value. The difference of watc-10-2304, 0.12, remains unexplained.

The results are those of the last run, with the version of each package and of HiGHS.

Outside the script, the reader was also checked on the eight multistage instances of Zehtabian and Bastin (CIRRELT-2016-12, Table 2: KW3R, KW3Rb, app0110R, SGPF3Y3, Asset Mgt, SGPF5Y4, wat10I16, wat10C32), against the values of the report, two of whose files (KW3Rb, a variant of KW3R with random technology matrices, and Asset Mgt) are not distributed with SPLIB or COIN-OR's Data-Stochastic. wat10I16 is SPLIB's WATSON instance wati-10-16, which the script checks; SMI's original stoch file of wat10I16, with its random bounds, gives the same value.

COIN-OR SMI 0.96.1, another reader, gives the same deterministic equivalents as this one on the files it reads: KandW3R, app0110R and wat10C32, and pltexpA46, pltexpA56 and pltexpB3 to pltexpB5 with their BLOCKS rewritten as SCENARIOS (SMI reads neither BLOCKS nor the bounds that the scenarios of SPLIB's WATSON files modify). Their optimal values agree, to the accuracy of the solvers (on pltexpA56, HiGHS gives the same value on both, Clp 1.6e-4 more on SMI's), once two differences of SMI are accounted for: it normalizes the probabilities (app0110R, whose nine probabilities 0.111 sum up to 0.999, gives 42 rather than 41.958), and it takes the entries that a scenario does not list, in its branch period and later, from the core file rather than from its parent (app0110R gives 44.667 and wat10C_32 -2622.062 until these entries are listed).

StochOptFormat

validation/sof.jl checks write_sof on the same instances, with readers other than SMPSFormat's: each instance with a published optimal value is written in StochOptFormat, read back by SDDP.jl (SDDP.read_from_file, which reads the subproblems with MathOptInterface's reader of MathOptFormat), and the deterministic equivalent SDDP.jl builds of its policy graph (SDDP.deterministic_equivalent) solved with HiGHS; its optimal value is compared with the published one as above. Each instance is written both ways: with random variables, unless it has random coefficients in its rows, which SDDP.jl does not read, and with the data of each node. lands3-indep, whose balance of period 3 is known in period 2, is written as its scenario tree.

julia --project=validation validation/sof.jl --size small --output sof-results.md

The options are those of validate.jl; the continuous integration runs the small instances. On them, the results are those of validate.jl, to the digits printed: every instance agrees with its published value, but for the known differences above, found again with the same values. Run up to the size medium, the first 79 instances, as far as rand0-2000, give the same verdicts; SDDP.jl's deterministic equivalents of the larger ones, with their copies of the state variables and their random variables fixed, take HiGHS much longer than SMPSFormat's.
